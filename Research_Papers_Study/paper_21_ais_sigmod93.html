Mining Association Rules between Sets of Items in Large Databases

Rakesh Agrawal, Tomasz Imielinski, Arun Swami (SIGMOD 1993)

⏱️ Focus: 15:00
1. The Historical Genesis

The Paper That Founded Association Rule Mining

In 1993, Rakesh Agrawal, Tomasz Imielinski, and Arun Swami published Mining Association Rules between Sets of Items in Large Databases at ACM SIGMOD. This landmark paper introduced the terms Support, Confidence, and Market Basket Analysis to computer science.

🛒 The Problem Formalization: Given a set of items $I = \{i_1, i_2, \dots, i_m\}$ and a database of transactions $D$, find all rules $X \implies Y$ such that: 1. Support: The percentage of transactions that contain $X \cup Y$ is $\ge \t\text{minsup}$. 2. Confidence: The percentage of transactions containing $X$ that ALSO contain $Y$ is $\ge \text{minconf}$.
2. The AIS Algorithm & Its Fatal Bottleneck

Why Did AIS Suffer From Candidate Explosion?

To understand why Apriori (1994) became so famous, you must understand how AIS worked in 1993 and why it ran out of memory.

❌ How AIS Generated Candidates:

In pass $k$, AIS reads transaction $T$. It checks which frequent $(k-1)$-itemsets $l \in L_{k-1}$ are present in $T$. For each such $l$, it pairs $l$ with EVERY OTHER ITEM present in $T$!

If transaction $T$ contains 40 items and matches 10 frequent sets, AIS immediately creates hundreds of candidates inside memory for that single basket, even if those items have never appeared together anywhere else!

✓ The Apriori 1994 Revolution:

Apriori recognized: Never look at raw transactions to generate candidates!

Instead, generate candidates globally using apriori-gen strictly from $L_{k-1} owtie L_{k-1}$, and immediately prune any candidate with an infrequent subset. Apriori generates 100x fewer candidates than AIS!

4. Active Retention Quiz

Test Your Understanding

1. Where does the AIS algorithm generate candidate itemsets?

Explanation: Correct! AIS generated candidates on the fly while scanning transactions, leading to enormous memory bloat when transactions were wide.

2. If Support({A, B}) = 10% and Support({A}) = 20%, what is Confidence(A ➔ B)?

Explanation: Correct! Confidence measures conditional probability P(B|A) = Support(A ∪ B) / Support(A).

3. What was the SETM algorithm developed alongside AIS?

Explanation: Correct! SETM used SQL table joins for candidate generation, but generated huge intermediate tables.

4. Why does Apriori (1994) dominate AIS (1993) in runtime and memory?

Explanation: Correct! Apriori's prune step discards candidates whose subsets are not frequent before even counting them in transactions.

5. Can confidence alone prove that two items are truly positively correlated?

Explanation: Correct! This limitation led to Lift, Chi-Square, and Kulczynski measures in later years.
