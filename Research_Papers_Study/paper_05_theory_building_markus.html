1. Beyond Data Snacking

Correlation is Not Causation: The Epistemology of Scientific Theory

In the Big Data era, machine learning algorithms can easily find correlations: for example, US cheese consumption correlates 94% with deaths by bedsheet tangling! Data mining without theoretical grounding produces dangerous illusions.

🧠 Theory as the Anchor: M. Lynne Markus and Carol S. Saunders (MIS Quarterly 2007) guide researchers on how to build sound theoretical models. A real theory does not simply report what correlated; it explains why, specifies boundary conditions, and formalizes mechanisms of causality.
2. Modes of Scientific Reasoning

Deduction vs. Induction vs. Abduction

Deductive Reasoning

Rule + Case ➔ Result.
Top-down: Start with established scientific axioms, deduce hypotheses, and test them with data. Guaranteed logic validity.

Inductive Reasoning

Case + Result ➔ Probable Rule.
Bottom-up (Data Mining): Observe 1,000,000 transactions and induce general patterns. Prone to spurious overfit.

Abductive Reasoning

Surprise Result + Rule ➔ Plausible Case.
Inference to the best explanation: Formulate creative theoretical hypotheses that make surprising data expected.

4. Active Retention Quiz

Test Your Understanding

1. What is the fundamental flaw of relying exclusively on inductive data mining without theory?

Explanation: Correct! Theory provides the causal 'why' that prevents being fooled by co-occurrence.

2. What reasoning mode describes formulating a creative explanatory hypothesis to account for a surprising data anomaly?

Explanation: Correct! Abduction is the engine of scientific insight generation.

3. What is a 'boundary condition' in a theoretical model?

Explanation: Correct! Every law has boundaries outside which it no longer applies.

4. Why does confounding (lurking variable) invalidate apparent causal rules?

Explanation: Correct! The classic example is outdoor summer temperature causing both ice cream consumption and swimming pool drownings.

5. How does a strong theoretical model benefit practical decision-making?

Explanation: Correct! True causal theories allow predicting the consequences of interventions.
