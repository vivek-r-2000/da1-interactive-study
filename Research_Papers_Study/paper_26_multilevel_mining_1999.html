Mining Multi-Level Association Rules in Large Databases

Jiawei Han & Yongjian Fu (IEEE TKDE 1999)

⏱️ Focus: 15:00
1. The Retail Taxonomy Dilemma

Why Single-Level Association Mining Misses Real Patterns

Real-world supermarket goods naturally form concept hierarchies: Food ➔ Dairy ➔ Milk ➔ 2% Low-Fat Darigold Milk.

⚠️ The Uniform Threshold Failure: If you set a single minimum support threshold (say, 5%):
1. At high concept levels (Milk, Bread): Support is high (30%), but rules are generic and obvious ("People who buy dairy buy food"). 2. At low concept levels (Darigold 2% 1-Gallon): Support is 0.05%. With a 5% threshold, every single specific product pattern is pruned away as infrequent!

Jiawei Han and Yongjian Fu (IEEE TKDE 1999) solved this by inventing Multi-Level Association Rule Mining using Reduced/Ascending Minimum Support and Encoded Transaction Tables ($T[1], T[2]$).
2. Theory & Encoding Architecture

Encoded Tables (T[1], T[2]) & Redundancy Filtering

Rather than repeatedly looking up hierarchical trees, Han & Fu encode every item using taxonomic prefixes.

Taxonomic Prefix Encoding:
• 111: Food ➔ Dairy ➔ Milk ➔ 2% Low-Fat
• 112: Food ➔ Dairy ➔ Cheese ➔ Cheddar
• 121: Food ➔ Bakery ➔ Bread ➔ Wheat

Table Filtering Optimization:
• Level 1: Filter raw database to create T[1] keeping only items frequent at Level 1.
• Level 2: Scan ONLY T[1] to produce T[2], drastically shrinking the scan size for lower levels!
✂️ Redundancy Filtering Rule: Suppose we discover Level 1 rule: Milk ➔ Bread (Confidence: 70%).
Later at Level 2, we find: 2% Milk ➔ Wheat Bread (Confidence: 71%).
Is the Level 2 rule useful? NO! It is almost identical in confidence to what the ancestor already established. The Level 2 rule is marked redundant and pruned unless its confidence differs significantly from the expected ancestor baseline!

4. Active Retention Quiz

Test Your Understanding

1. Why does uniform minimum support fail in multi-level association mining?

Explanation: Correct! General categories accumulate the transactions of all their children, so they need higher thresholds than specific leaf items.

2. What is an Encoded Transaction Table T[1] in Han & Fu's framework?

Explanation: Correct! T[1] shrinks the database so that mining deeper level patterns does not require scanning the full raw dataset.

3. When is a lower-level rule considered REDUNDANT?

Explanation: Correct! If Milk ➔ Bread has 70% confidence, learning that Darigold Milk ➔ Wheat Bread also has 70% confidence tells us nothing new.

4. Can an association rule cross different levels (e.g., specific item ➔ general category)?

Explanation: Correct! Cross-level mining connects specific niche items with general categories.

5. Why do we encode taxonomies with prefixes like '111', '112'?

Explanation: Correct! Checking if 111 and 112 share a category only requires checking if their first two digits match ('11').
