Mining Quantitative Association Rules in Large Relational Tables

Ramakrishnan Srikant & Rakesh Agrawal (SIGMOD 1996)

⏱️ Focus: 15:00
1. The Quantitative Catch-22

Mining Continuous Attributes in Relational Databases

Traditional association rules deal with binary flags: buys_beer = 1. But real database records contain continuous numeric fields: Age: 34, Income: $78,500, Married: 1.

⚠️ The MinSup vs. MinConf Catch-22 Dilemma: To mine rules like $\langle\text{Age: } 25..35 angle \land \langle\text{Income: } \$50k..\$80k angle \implies \langle\text{NumCars: } 2 \rangle$, we must partition numbers into intervals:

1. If intervals are too narrow (e.g. Age: 25-26, 27-28): Each interval contains very few rows. Support falls below $\t\text{minsup}$, and genuine patterns are pruned away! 2. If intervals are too wide (e.g. Age: 0-50): Support is high, but confidence drops and the discovered rule becomes completely useless and vague.

Ramakrishnan Srikant and Rakesh Agrawal (SIGMOD 1996) solved this fundamental dilemma with Equi-Depth Partitioning, Consecutive Interval Merging, and the Partial Completeness Measure.
2. Theoretical Solution & Partial Completeness

The 3-Step Quantitative Mining Algorithm

Srikant & Agrawal provide a mathematically rigorous solution ensuring no important pattern is missed:

Algorithm Overview:
1. Equi-Depth Partitioning: Divide each continuous attribute into N intervals where each interval contains exactly the same number of tuples (1/N of the dataset).

2. Consecutive Merging: Combine up to K adjacent intervals into compound intervals (e.g., [1], [2], [1,2], [2,3], [1,2,3]) as long as combined support doesn't exceed maximum support.

3. Partial Completeness (R):
If an un-discretized real-world rule has support S and confidence C, Srikant & Agrawal prove their discretized merging guarantees finding a rule with support ≥ S/R and confidence ≥ C/R!
🔍 Pruning Redundant Merged Intervals: Merging consecutive intervals creates an explosion of overlapping candidate rules. A merged interval $[1..3]$ is pruned as uninteresting if its confidence is not significantly greater than its sub-intervals $[1..2]$ or $[2..3]$.

4. Active Retention Quiz

Test Your Understanding

1. What is the fundamental Catch-22 dilemma of quantitative association mining?

Explanation: Correct! The dilemma balances pattern frequency (support) against pattern predictive purity (confidence).

2. Why does Srikant & Agrawal use Equi-Depth binning instead of Equi-Width binning?

Explanation: Correct! Skewed data clusters in certain ranges; equi-depth ensures statistical representation across all bins.

3. What does Partial Completeness (R) quantify?

Explanation: Correct! It mathematically guarantees that discretization does not miss genuine rules beyond factor R.

4. Why do Srikant & Agrawal only merge CONSECUTIVE intervals, not arbitrary subsets?

Explanation: Correct! Continuous quantitative concepts correspond to connected intervals.

5. How are redundant merged intervals pruned?

Explanation: Correct! If widening an interval adds tuples without increasing predictive confidence, the wider interval is pruned as redundant.
