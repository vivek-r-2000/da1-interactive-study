Sequential Dependencies

Lukasz Golab, Howard Karloff, Flip Korn et al. (VLDB 2009)

⏱️ Focus: 15:00
1. Beyond Static Relational Constraints

Capturing Time, Sequence, and Delays in Big Data Logs

Traditional Functional Dependencies (FDs) such as ZipCode ➔ City are static. They state: if two tuples have the same ZipCode, they must have the same City.

⏱️ The Temporal Void: Modern data streams (server access logs, electronic health records, stock trades) are ordered by time. Static FDs cannot express real-world temporal integrity constraints such as:

• "A patient undergoing Chemotherapy A cannot receive Radiation B within 48 hours."
• "An ATM withdrawal in London cannot occur within 30 minutes of a withdrawal in Tokyo by the same customer."
• "In an online checkout, payment confirmation cannot happen more than 15 minutes after cart checkout initiation."

Lukasz Golab, Howard Karloff, Flip Korn, Avi Saha, and Divesh Srivastava (VLDB 2009) formalized Sequential Dependencies (SDs) to bring order and gap semantics into database theory.
2. Mathematical Formalism

Sequential Dependencies (SDs) with Gap Constraints

An SD specifies a relationship over pairs of tuples that are ordered according to a sequence attribute (e.g. timestamp $T$).

Formal Sequential Dependency Definition:
SD: (X ≤ g_max) ∧ (Y ≥ g_min) ⇒ Z

For any two consecutive or windowed tuples t1, t2 in sequence order T:
If: t2.CustID = t1.CustID AND (t2.Time - t1.Time ≤ ΔT)
Then: t2.Location = t1.Location (No impossible teleportation!)
🔍 Anomaly Detection via SD Violations: Instead of training complex black-box neural networks for fraud, evaluating SD constraints over incoming streams catches fraud deterministically in microseconds!

4. Active Retention Quiz

Test Your Understanding

1. What is the fundamental difference between standard FDs and Sequential Dependencies (SDs)?

Explanation: Correct! SDs generalize FDs by modeling sequence order (e.g. timestamp) and allowable metric intervals.

2. In an SD, what does a gap constraint [g_min, g_max] specify?

Explanation: Correct! Gap bounds define the time or sequence window across which the constraint is actively enforced.

3. How can SDs be applied in financial fraud detection?

Explanation: Correct! Impossible velocity / distance teleportation directly violates sequential constraints.

4. What is the computational complexity of verifying SDs over a sorted log stream?

Explanation: Correct! Because records are sorted by sequence, an active sliding window can check incoming tuples in linear time.

5. Can Sequential Dependencies be mined automatically from historical logs?

Explanation: Correct! Automatic SD discovery uncovers latent workflow rules without manual specification.
