1. Historical Epistemology

Fifty Years of Classification and Regression Trees

Decision trees are ubiquitous in AI. However, textbooks often present CART (1984) and C4.5 (1993) as if they appeared out of nowhere and are flawless.

⚠️ The Dirty Secret of Classic Trees: Algorithms that maximize Information Gain or Gini (CART, C4.5) suffer from severe Variable Selection Bias! Attributes with many categories or continuous values are statistically favored to be chosen as splits, even when they are completely uninformative random noise!

Wei-Yin Loh (International Statistical Review 2014) at the University of Wisconsin-Madison surveyed 50 years of decision tree algorithms (from AID in 1963 to CART, QUEST, CRUISE, and GUIDE), proving how modern statistical split tests solve selection bias.
2. Mathematical Formalism

Variable Selection Bias & Chi-Square Unbiased Splitting

Why Gini and Entropy Suffer from Selection Bias:
If attribute X_1 has k distinct values, there are 2^{k-1} - 1 possible binary splits.
• Feature A (Binary: Yes/No): Only 1 possible split.
• Feature B (20 Categories): 2^{19} - 1 = 524,287 possible splits!
By random chance alone, Feature B is 500,000x more likely to find a split that reduces Gini impurity purely by memorizing sample noise!

The Unbiased Solution (QUEST & GUIDE):
Separate Variable Selection from Split Point Selection!
1. Select the splitting variable using Chi-Square p-values (independent of number of categories).
2. Once the variable is chosen, find its optimal split point. Zero selection bias!

Explanation: Correct! More candidate split points artificially inflate maximum Gini reduction.

2. How did QUEST (Loh & Shih 1997) eliminate variable selection bias?

Explanation: Correct! Statistical significance tests choose variables without bias from category cardinalities.

3. What was the earliest regression tree algorithm (1963)?

Explanation: Correct! AID launched recursive partitioning in social science survey analysis in 1963.

4. What breakthrough pruning method was introduced by CART in 1984?

Explanation: Correct! Cost-complexity pruning balanced misclassification cost against tree complexity.

5. What modern feature does the GUIDE algorithm introduce for regression trees?

Explanation: Correct! Fitting linear regression models inside leaf partitions captures continuous trends accurately.
